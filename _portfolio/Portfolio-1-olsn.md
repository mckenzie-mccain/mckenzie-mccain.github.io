<!-- RAD LAB APPLICAITON: A project I'm proud of is the OLSN project where I helped design a new prosthetic hand for a local child born with Amniotic Band Syndrome. Previously, he had been made a prosthetic hand from a group of high-schoolers, but the most significant issue was that his rate of growth caused him to constantly outgrow his prostheses. The OLSN project has taken on this challenge of making a 'growable' prosthetic as he ages. This was done through the use of SolidWorks design tables to parameterize the model. 

My contribution was to take measurements of the kid's hand to input the dimensions into a SolidWorks design table to constrain the finger segments to mirror his growth alongside him. Furthermore, I created subassemblies for each finger using configuration tables which were then implemented into a full assembly of the mechanical hand. The fingers include a hexagonal-mesh which improved the adhesion of silicone to the 3d-printed PAHT-CF finger segments.

Additionally, through iteration, my contribution to the hand helped design a two-way string/tension system to flex and extend the fingers. This was done through two ratchet mechanisms to control the fingers and attach the forearm band. 

This project stands out as the most impactful for my growth as an engineer, this is because I learned many technical skills related to mechanical engineering such as design tables, parameterization, configurations, and assemblies/subassemblies. On another note, this project is especially meaningful because it applied my technical skills in a way that directly benefited someone within my community, helping a low-income family have access to resources to build a prosthetic hand that often costs thousands of dollars. -->
